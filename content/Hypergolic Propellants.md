>[!Definition] Hypergolic propellant
>A mixture of rocket propellants where the components *spontaneously ignite each other under contact*

A popular hypergolic propellant is **dinitrogen tetroxide** and **UDMH (Unsymmetrical Dimethylhydrazine)**

Hypergolic mixtures are easy to store in liquid form and are easy to ignite due to their properties. However, the chemicals themselves can be toxic and corrosive in nature. ISRO's [[Vikas Engine Restart Test - ISRO|Vikas engine]] uses N2O4 + UDMH
# Modern use
* Hypergolic fuels aren't used commonly because of the toxic and corrosive nature of the components themselves. It can be nasty to handle them and is also not eco-friendly
* They don't deliver as much performance compared to common propellants like [[RP1 + LOX]]
* Hypergolics are also more expensive

However, their advantage of simple contact ignition makes them useful for sudden thrust requirements
